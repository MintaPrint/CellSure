How to check a used EV's battery health before you buy.

Last updated · 15 November 2026

The number to ask for is the battery's State of Health (SoH): how much energy the battery holds now, as a percentage of what it held when new. You cannot see it by looking at the car. The reliable way to get it is an independent battery health test, from someone with no stake in the sale.

What is battery State of Health?

State of Health is the battery's usable capacity today, compared with when it was new. A 50 kWh battery at 90% SoH now holds about 45 kWh, so expect roughly 10% less range than the car had when new.

It is not the same as the charge level on the dash. Charge level tells you how full the battery is right now. State of Health tells you how big the battery still is.

All EV batteries lose capacity with age and use. How fast depends on the car and how it has been used and charged. That is why you need the figure for the exact car, not an average.

What is a good State of Health for a used EV?

There is no single pass mark. Judge the figure against the car's age, mileage and price.

One line is worth knowing. Most makers cover the battery for around 8 years or 100,000 miles, and will repair or replace it if capacity falls below about 70% in that time. Terms vary by maker and model, so check the warranty for the exact car.

Your CellSure certificate gives a plain-English result for the car, so you know where it stands.

Why the dashboard is not enough

The range on the dash is an estimate. It moves with temperature and recent driving, so the same battery can show very different numbers on different days. It does not tell you the battery's State of Health.

Common questions

Can I trust the range shown on the dashboard?

No. The range figure is an estimate that moves with temperature and recent driving, so the same battery can show very different numbers on different days. It does not tell you the battery's State of Health.

What State of Health is too low?

Most makers' battery warranties promise to repair or replace the battery if it falls below about 70% within the warranty period. Above that, judge the figure against the car's age, mileage and price.

Does the seller have to agree to a battery test?

Yes. The test needs access to the car, so the owner has to allow it. CellSure meets you at the seller's location, private or dealer.
